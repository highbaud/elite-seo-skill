# Elite SEO Knowledge Plan

Date: 2026-07-06

> **Scope:** the strategy and operating model: what to build, in what order, and how to outrank and defend visibility over 90 days. For diagnosing a specific site, use the audit modules in `elite-seo-audit-strategy-expansion.md`; for step-by-step execution, use the SOPs indexed in `kit/README.md`.

This project plan turns the supplied SEO/outbound source folder into a repeatable system for ranking, outranking, and defending search visibility across Google, Bing, ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, social SERPs, local packs, image/video search, and review-led buyer research.

It intentionally separates durable SEO from short-lived arbitrage. Several supplied sources promote "parasite SEO", mass content, paid links, domain mimicry, and fake/low-quality review plays. Those are useful as market signals, but they are not the foundation of this plan. Google explicitly treats ranking manipulation, link spam, scaled content abuse, scraping, and site reputation abuse as spam risks. OpenAI also states there is no guaranteed way to rank in ChatGPT Search; inclusion depends in part on allowing OAI-SearchBot and producing reliable, relevant public content.

## Executive Summary

The strongest plan is not "publish everywhere and hope." It is a layered authority system.

Layer 1: Owned domain authority. Build technically clean, crawlable, fast, credible pages that answer valuable queries better than the current winners.

Layer 2: Entity and reputation graph. Make the brand, product, people, reviews, comparisons, videos, images, social profiles, and third-party mentions consistent across the web so search engines and answer engines can confidently associate the entity with the topic.

Layer 3: Borrowed authority distribution. Use LinkedIn, Reddit, YouTube, Medium, GitHub, Substack, Notion, Quora, SlideShare, Pinterest, and other high-authority platforms as discovery, trust, and citation surfaces. Do not make these the permanent asset base.

Layer 4: Link earning and relationship engine. Most competitors publish content. Fewer build real links, mentions, reviews, citations, and expert collaborations. This is the durable arbitrage.

Layer 5: Measurement and refresh. Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, analytics, rank tracking, server logs, and AI citation tests feed a weekly improvement loop.

The goal is to become the most useful and most cited answer for a topic, then make that answer available in every format and surface where buyers, journalists, communities, and AI systems look.

### 1. Parasite SEO and High-Authority Platforms

Sources included the Parasite SEO Guide docx and posts about LinkedIn, Reddit, Medium, GitHub Pages, Notion, Substack, Quora, Dev.to, SlideShare, Pinterest, Behance, Academia.edu, Hackernoon, Issuu, Gumroad, Loom, Typeform, Carrd, Figma Community, Apple Notes, and Patreon.

Useful signal:

- High-authority platforms can rank fast because they already have crawl frequency, link equity, trust, and user engagement.
- Platforms work best when the content matches native platform expectations.
- A new profile should publish several helpful posts before adding commercial links.
- Platform pages can test SERP angles before committing to owned-domain content.
- Platform content can occupy branded, review, comparison, and long-tail SERP real estate.

Risk:

- If the main goal is to exploit a host site's reputation rather than help users, it can overlap with site reputation abuse or spam patterns.
- Platform rules change. A platform that ranks today can be devalued, noindexed, deleted, or algorithmically ignored.
- Repeated spun or duplicated assets across platforms can become thin, low-value content.

Durable adaptation:

- Treat third-party platforms as satellites, not the base.
- Keep canonical, deepest content on the owned domain.
- Use platform-native summaries, proofs, demos, or discussions that link back naturally.
- Track each platform asset as a temporary SERP asset with an expiration date.

### 2. AI Search, AEO, GEO, and "Ranking on ChatGPT"

Sources included tools and posts about ChatGPT citation checks, ranking for competitor brand names, "alternatives" pages, listicles, AI SEO trackers, Surfer AI Tracker, SEOPro AI, Search Atlas, MagicSpace, Soogle, and Notion content about ranking in ChatGPT.

Useful signal:

- AI answers often pull from indexed web content, high-authority lists, review pages, product docs, comparison content, forums, YouTube, and sources with clear entity associations.
- "Best X", "X alternatives", "X vs Y", "is X legit", "X review", "pricing", "pros and cons", and "for [use case]" queries are high-value answer-engine surfaces.
- Listicles and comparison pages matter because models and search systems need structured options.
- Brand mentions without links can still help entity recognition and buyer trust, though the exact search impact varies by system.

Verified constraints:

- OpenAI says ChatGPT Search has no guaranteed top placement and is based on factors intended to find reliable, relevant information.
- Sites should allow OAI-SearchBot if they want to be included in ChatGPT search summaries/snippets.
- OpenAI says ChatGPT Search referral traffic includes `utm_source=chatgpt.com`.
- Perplexity documents `PerplexityBot` for search result surfacing and `Perplexity-User` for user-triggered page visits.
- Google says generative AI search still depends on the same core crawling, indexing, snippets, technical structure, helpfulness, and spam-policy constraints.

Durable adaptation:

- Build "answer-ready" pages with concise definitions, comparison tables, original experience, structured data, citations, author/entity details, and updated facts.
- Allow relevant AI search crawlers unless there is a business reason not to.
- Create query-specific pages only when they have distinct user value. Do not generate thousands of near-duplicate AI pages.
- Track citations manually and through analytics, not through claims from a single tool.

### 3. Reviews, Reputation, and Consideration-Stage SEO

Sources included Edward Sturm review articles, posts about Google reviews/local ranking, review sites summarizing review sites, and "is X legit" tactics.

Useful signal:

- Buyers often search reviews outside the official website.
- Positive reviews should not sit in one silo. They should be converted into search-visible assets: website testimonials, YouTube clips, LinkedIn posts, Facebook posts, review pages, case studies, short videos, and PR/news-style updates.
- Review content should use the language buyers search: "[brand] review", "[brand] testimonials", "is [brand] legit", "[brand] rating", "[brand] vs [competitor]".
- Video testimonials with transcripts are valuable because they are both human-trust assets and machine-readable text.

Risk:

- Fake reviews, undisclosed incentives, fabricated customer claims, and misleading third-party-looking review sites are reputational and legal risks.
- Separate domains created only to manipulate branded SERPs can look suspicious if they pretend to be independent.

Durable adaptation:

- Create a transparent review hub on the owned domain first: `/reviews/`, `/testimonials/`, or `/customers/`.
- Add a branded review summary page if useful, but label it clearly as brand-owned.
- Publish real customer quotes with permission, context, customer type, date, media, and transcript.
- Add review schema only when it follows Google's structured data rules and the review content is visible to users.
- Use testimonials in retargeting and sales pages, but keep claims accurate.

### 4. Link Building and Outbound

Sources included Troy's link-building arbitrage post, tier-2 link services, Reddit link-building services, LinkDR, FatJoe brand mentions, cold-email system screenshots, DeliverOn, cold-email stack posts, and outbound automation posts.

Useful signal:

- Many sites now publish content but neglect real promotion and link acquisition.
- Linkable assets win links better than generic blog posts.
- Outbound works when the target has a real reason to care: broken link replacement, original data, expert quote, useful tool, local resource, visual asset, customer story, or partner mention.
- Email infrastructure matters for deliverability when doing legitimate outreach at scale.

Risk:

- Paid links intended to pass ranking credit, link farms, tiered spam links, mass guest posts, and irrelevant brand mentions can trigger search quality issues.
- Cold outbound can damage domains and reputation if volume exceeds relevance.

Durable adaptation:

- Build a relationship-driven link engine.
- Use paid promotion for visibility, not undisclosed ranking manipulation.
- For compensated placements, use appropriate `rel="sponsored"` or `rel="nofollow"` where applicable.
- Track link quality by topical relevance, editorial control, traffic, indexation, and relationship value, not just DR.

### 5. Programmatic SEO and Long-Tail Scale

Sources included the programmatic SEO post about 100k pages, long-tail AI posts, sitemap exploit references, ChatGPT-agent keyword searches, and Search Console regex screenshots.

Useful signal:

- Long-tail pages can work when there is a real data model and each page answers a distinct intent.
- Search Console regex can reveal question queries and long-tail impressions already connected to the site.
- Programmatic content needs templates, data quality, internal linking, index control, and refresh logic.

Risk:

- Google defines scaled content abuse as many pages generated primarily to manipulate rankings without helping users.
- Sitemap manipulation, thin AI pages, city/service swaps, and scraped pages are high-risk.

Durable adaptation:

- Only scale pages where each page has unique data, unique usefulness, and a clear reason to exist.
- Use `noindex` for thin, incomplete, filtered, or low-demand programmatic pages.
- Start with 50 to 200 pages, measure, then expand.
- Add human review to templates before indexation.

### 6. Technical SEO and Site Architecture

Sources included LibreCrawl, Screaming Frog custom extraction, URL simplification, content pruning, schema posts, image SEO, and sitemap references.

Useful signal:

- Crawlability, indexability, canonicalization, internal links, URL structure, schema, page speed, and pruning have compounding impact.
- Removing weak pages can improve average site quality and crawl allocation when the pages are truly low-value.
- Shorter, descriptive URLs are often better for users and maintenance, but URL changes must be handled carefully with redirects.
- Custom extraction can find stale years, outdated claims, missing schema, broken metadata, thin sections, and old offers at scale.

Risk:

- Deleting or changing URLs without mapping backlinks, traffic, rankings, and redirects can destroy value.
- Schema does not guarantee rich results or ranking.

Durable adaptation:

- Run a technical baseline before publishing or pruning.
- Classify every URL: keep, improve, consolidate, redirect, noindex, delete.
- Use schema to clarify visible content, not to fake relevance.

5. Distribution network: Content appears on owned, social, community, video, document, and high-authority platform surfaces.

## Phase 0: Rules of Engagement

Before doing anything, define the risk boundary.

Allowed:

- Original, useful content.
- Transparent comparisons.
- Real reviews with permission.
- Editorial outreach.
- Digital PR.
- Helpful community participation.
- Structured data that matches visible content.
- Programmatic pages with unique data and user value.
- Platform content that is native to the platform.
- Paid ads for traffic and retargeting.

Avoid:

- Fake reviews.
- Fake third-party review sites.
- Link farms.
- Paid dofollow links sold as "SEO power."
- Hidden links.
- Cloaking.
- Scraped pages.
- AI-spun pages at scale.
- Doorway pages.
- Competitor domain impersonation.
- Search-result scraping against Google ToS.
- Sitemap/indexing "exploits."

Risk-coded approach:

- Green: durable and recommended.
- Yellow: test with controls and transparency.
- Red: do not build a real business on it.

Parasite SEO is yellow when it is platform-native and useful. It becomes red when the purpose is only to exploit host authority.

## Phase 1: Baseline Audit

### 1. Crawl the Site

Use Screaming Frog, Sitebulb, LibreCrawl, Search Atlas, or another crawler.

Collect:

- All indexable URLs.
- Status codes.
- Canonicals.
- Redirect chains.
- Title tags.
- Meta descriptions.
- H1/H2 structure.
- Word count.
- Internal link count.
- Orphan pages.
- Sitemap inclusion.
- Robots directives.
- Schema types.
- Images without alt text.
- Large images.
- Core Web Vitals/PageSpeed data.
- Duplicate or near-duplicate pages.
- Dates in titles/content.
- Old claims, old years, outdated pricing, expired offers.

### 2. Search Console and Bing Webmaster Baseline

Export:

- Queries by clicks.
- Queries by impressions.
- Pages by clicks.
- Pages by impressions.
- Pages with high impressions and low CTR.
- Pages with declining clicks.
- Pages ranking positions 4-15.
- Indexed vs not indexed.
- Crawl stats.
- Manual actions/security issues.

Set filters:

- Question queries:

```regex
^(who|what|where|when|why|how|can|should|does|do|is|are|will|would|which|best|vs|compare|review|alternative|pricing)
```

- Long-tail queries with 7+ words:

```regex
(\b\w+\b\s){7,}
```

- Consideration keywords:

```regex
(review|reviews|rating|ratings|testimonial|testimonials|legit|scam|alternative|alternatives|vs|versus|pricing|cost|best|top|compare|comparison)
```

- Local intent:

```regex
(near me|nearby|in [a-z ]+|best [a-z ]+ in|open now|service area|local)
```

Google Search Console uses RE2 syntax and partial matching by default, so anchor with `^` and `$` when needed.

### 3. AI Search Baseline

Run the same prompts in ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, Google AI results, Bing Copilot if available, and regular Google/Bing.

Track:

- Prompt.
- Engine.
- Date.
- Location.
- Answer.
- Sources cited.
- Competitors cited.
- Whether your site appears.
- Whether your brand is mentioned but not linked.
- Missing facts.
- Wrong facts.
- Follow-up query generated by the answer.

Core prompts:

- "Best [category] for [use case]"
- "[brand] review"
- "Is [brand] legit?"
- "[brand] alternatives"

## Phase 3: Technical Foundation

### Technical Checklist

Must pass:

- Site is crawlable.
- Important pages are indexable.
- XML sitemap includes canonical indexable URLs only.
- Robots.txt does not block important sections.
- HTTP status codes are clean.
- One canonical version of each URL.
- HTTPS everywhere.
- Mobile responsive.
- Core templates are fast.
- Internal links expose important pages.
- Navigation reflects real topical architecture.
- No orphan money pages.
- No accidental noindex.
- No duplicate title tags on important pages.
- No broken internal links.
- No redirect chains on important URLs.
- Images use standard `<img>` or fallback `src` attributes where indexation matters.
- Key images have descriptive alt text and relevant surrounding copy.
- Video pages include relevant text, title, description, and transcript.

### URL Policy

Good URL:

```text
/best-crm-for-roofers/
/xero-alternatives/
/locations/austin-roof-repair/
/reviews/
/case-studies/acme-reduced-onboarding-time/
```

Avoid:

```text
/blog/2024/03/09/post-title/
/p?id=123
/best-crm-best-crm-software-best-crm-tools/
```

Changing URLs can help users and maintenance, but only change URLs when the gain justifies redirect risk.

Before changing:

- Export backlinks.
- Export traffic.
- Export rankings.
- Map old to new.
- Use 301 redirects.
- Update internal links.
- Update sitemap.
- Monitor 30-60 days.

### Content Pruning

Classify every weak page:

- Keep: already performs or is strategically necessary.
- Improve: has impressions, backlinks, conversions, or topical need.
- Consolidate: overlaps with stronger page.
- Redirect: has links/traffic but poor standalone value.
- Noindex: useful to users but not useful in search.
- Delete: no traffic, no links, no business purpose.

Do not delete pages because they are old. Delete or consolidate because they are low-value, redundant, misleading, thin, or harmful to crawl/index quality.

### Prioritization Formula

Score 1-5:

- Business value.
- Ranking feasibility.
- Existing traction.
- Conversion intent.
- Content uniqueness.
- Linkability.
- AI citation potential.

Priority score:

```text
(business value + conversion intent + existing traction + content uniqueness + AI citation potential) - difficulty
```

## Phase 6: Programmatic SEO That Does Not Collapse

### When Programmatic SEO Makes Sense

Use it when:

- There is a real repeatable data model.
- Each page answers a distinct query.
- Each page has unique data or analysis.
- Pages can be internally linked cleanly.
- You can maintain/update the data.
- You can index selectively.

Good examples:

- "[software] alternatives" pages with real feature/pricing data.
- "Cost of [service] in [city]" with real local pricing inputs.
- "[integration] setup guide" pages with actual API steps.
- "[industry] benchmark" pages from original data.
- "[product] vs [product]" with factual comparison.

Bad examples:

- 5,000 city pages with swapped city names.
- AI summaries of scraped SERPs.
- Pages created only because a keyword exists.
- Pages with no unique proof.

### Programmatic Launch Gate

Before indexing a template:

- Manually review 20 pages.
- Confirm unique fields render correctly.
- Confirm pages are useful without AI fluff.
- Confirm no duplicate titles/H1s.
- Confirm internal links.
- Confirm canonical logic.
- Confirm schema matches visible content.
- Confirm index/noindex logic.
- Confirm sitemap includes only indexable pages.
- Confirm page speed.
- Confirm analytics events.

Launch in batches:

- Batch 1: 25 pages.
- Batch 2: 100 pages after 2-4 weeks.
- Batch 3: 500 pages only after performance proof.

## Phase 9: AI Search and Citation System

### Crawler Access

Check `robots.txt` and CDN/firewall rules for:

```text
User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: GPTBot
Allow: / or Disallow: / depending on training policy

User-agent: PerplexityBot
Allow: /
```

OpenAI states OAI-SearchBot is used to surface sites in ChatGPT Search. GPTBot is separate and relates to training. Perplexity similarly documents PerplexityBot for surfacing/linking websites in Perplexity search results.

Two additional rules that prevent common mistakes: Google's AI Overviews/AI Mode are fed by normal Googlebot (blocking `Google-Extended` only opts out of Gemini training, it does not remove AI Overviews visibility), and ChatGPT Search leans partly on Bing's index, so Bing Webmaster Tools plus IndexNow count as AI-visibility work. ## Outranking Playbooks

### Playbook A: Outrank a Weak Informational Page

1. Identify page ranking 1-5 with weak freshness, thin examples, no visuals, or no original proof.
2. Build a page with a direct answer, better structure, examples, screenshots, data, and FAQ.
3. Add internal links from relevant pages.
4. Publish supporting LinkedIn/Medium/Substack summary.
5. Build 5-15 relevant links/mentions.
6. Monitor GSC impressions and update every 2 weeks.

1. Make a fair comparison page.
2. Do not pretend to be the competitor.
3. Include who should choose competitor vs you.
4. Add comparison table.
5. Add use-case-specific recommendations.
6. Add customer proof.
7. Add FAQ.
8. Create YouTube version.
9. Pitch inclusion in third-party alternatives/listicles.
10. Track AI answers for the competitor query.

1. Build `/reviews/`.
2. Add real testimonials and video transcripts.

5. Add review schema only if eligible.
